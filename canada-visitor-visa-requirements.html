CANADA VISA GUIDE

Canada Visitor Visa Requirements

A practical guide to preparing for a Canadian visitor visa application for tourism, family visits, or other permitted short-term visits.

A Canadian visitor visa is also called a Temporary Resident Visa (TRV). Whether you need a visitor visa or an Electronic Travel Authorization (eTA) depends on your nationality, travel document, and how you travel to Canada. Check the official requirements before applying.

Official starting point: Check whether you need a Canadian visa or eTA .

1. Basic eligibility requirements

To qualify for a visitor visa, you generally need to demonstrate that you:

  • Have a valid passport or other acceptable travel document.
  • Are in good health, and complete a medical examination if required.
  • Have no disqualifying criminal or immigration-related history.
  • Have sufficient money for your visit and return journey.
  • Have ties to your home country, such as employment, family, property, or other commitments.
  • Will leave Canada at the end of your authorized stay.
  • Meet any other requirements that apply to your circumstances.

Meeting the basic requirements does not guarantee approval. An immigration officer assesses each application individually.

2. Common supporting documents checklist

The exact document list depends on your circumstances. Use the official application process to obtain the checklist for your case. The following documents may be useful:

  • Passport: A clear copy of your valid passport and relevant pages.
  • Application information: The forms and information requested by the official application portal.
  • Travel purpose: A clear explanation of why you want to visit Canada.
  • Travel itinerary: Proposed travel dates, accommodation details, or other plans where relevant.
  • Proof of funds: Bank statements or other credible evidence showing how the trip will be paid for.
  • Employment or business evidence: For example, an employer letter, approved leave, or business documents, if applicable.
  • Home-country ties: Evidence of family responsibilities, employment, studies, property, or other commitments, where relevant.
  • Invitation letter: If visiting family, friends, or an organization, an invitation may support your application when relevant.
  • Travel history: Copies of previous visas and travel records, if available and relevant.
  • Family documents: Birth certificates, marriage certificates, consent letters, or custody documents when applicable.
  • Translations: Documents not in English or French may need an appropriate translation under IRCC rules.

Important: This is a general preparation checklist, not a universal mandatory list. Do not submit false, altered, or misleading documents.

3. How to apply for a Canada visitor visa

  1. Check your travel-document requirement. Confirm whether you need a visitor visa or an eTA.
  2. Choose the correct application route. Follow the official instructions for your purpose of travel and situation.
  3. Prepare your documents. Gather your passport, financial evidence, travel information, and any supporting documents requested.
  4. Complete the application carefully. Ensure that your names, dates, travel purpose, and other details are consistent with your documents.
  5. Pay the required fees. Check the current government fee schedule and whether a biometrics fee applies to you.
  6. Give biometrics if instructed. Follow the official instructions if fingerprints and a photograph are required.
  7. Monitor your application. Check your official account and respond promptly to requests for additional information.
  8. Follow passport-submission instructions if approved. Approval of an online application does not mean you can travel before any required visa has been issued.

4. If you are visiting family or friends

An invitation letter may explain who is inviting you, your relationship, the reason for the visit, where you may stay, and who will cover particular expenses. The person inviting you may also provide evidence of their status in Canada when relevant.

An invitation letter does not guarantee that a visa will be granted. You must still meet the applicable requirements.

5. Common application mistakes to avoid

  • Applying for the wrong travel document or visa category.
  • Submitting incomplete forms or missing requested documents.
  • Providing inconsistent travel dates or personal information.
  • Failing to explain the purpose of the visit clearly.
  • Providing financial evidence that does not reasonably support the planned trip.
  • Using false information or altered documents.
  • Ignoring requests from immigration authorities or biometrics instructions.
  • Assuming that payment or submission guarantees approval.

6. Fees and processing times

Government fees, biometrics requirements, and processing times can change. They may also depend on the type of application and the applicant's circumstances. Check the official Canadian government website for the current information before paying or making travel plans.

7. Important information for applicants

  • Visitor visas and eTAs are different travel authorizations.
  • A visitor visa does not automatically guarantee entry into Canada.
  • Most visitors are normally permitted to stay for up to six months, but the border officer can authorize a different period.
  • A visitor visa is not a work permit or study permit.
  • Use official Canadian government websites for the final requirements and application process.
