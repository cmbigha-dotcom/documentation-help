Business Document Requirements: What Documents Do You Need?

Business document requirements vary by country, business structure, industry and the government authorities responsible for registration and licensing. This guide explains common documents that may be needed when starting, registering or operating a business.

Common Business Documents

Depending on your business and location, you may be asked for some or all of the following:

  • Business or company registration application.
  • Identification documents for owners, directors or authorized representatives.
  • Business name or company name information.
  • Business address or proof of registered address.
  • Company formation documents, where required.
  • Tax registration or taxpayer identification documents.
  • Business permits or sector-specific licenses, where required.
  • Proof of payment of applicable official registration or licensing fees.

How Business Registration Usually Works

  1. Choose the appropriate business structure and name.
  2. Check the official registration requirements for your location.
  3. Prepare identification and business information.
  4. Complete the official registration application.
  5. Pay the applicable government fees.
  6. Receive your registration certificate or official business documents.
  7. Complete any required tax, licensing or sector-specific registrations.

Business Permits and Licenses

Some businesses require additional permits or licenses before they can operate. These may depend on the type of business, location, industry and activities involved.

Tax and Financial Documents

A registered business may need tax registration, taxpayer identification documents, invoices, financial records or other documents required by the relevant tax authority.

Important: Business Requirements Are Country-Specific

Registration procedures, fees, taxes, permits and eligibility rules differ between countries and can change over time. Always verify the current requirements with the official business registration, tax or licensing authority responsible for your application.
