Employment & Work Documents: What Do You Need?

Employment document requirements vary by country, employer, occupation and type of work. This guide explains documents commonly requested when applying for a job, starting employment or completing work authorization procedures.

Common Employment Documents

Depending on the employer and country, you may be asked for some or all of the following:

  • A valid identity document or passport.
  • A CV or résumé describing your education and work experience.
  • Academic certificates, diplomas or professional qualifications.
  • Employment references or previous employment letters.
  • A job offer or employment contract.
  • Passport photographs, where required.
  • Background, police or medical documents where legally required.
  • A work permit, employment authorization or appropriate visa for foreign workers.

Documents for Starting a New Job

After receiving a job offer, an employer may request additional information to verify your identity, qualifications, eligibility to work, payment details or other information required under local employment regulations.

Work Permits and Foreign Workers

People working outside their country of citizenship may need a work permit, employment authorization or work visa before beginning employment. The exact procedure depends on the destination country, nationality, employer and type of work.

Qualifications and Professional Documents

Some professions require proof of qualifications, professional licences, certifications or registration with a regulatory authority. Certain foreign qualifications may also need verification or official recognition.

Important: Requirements Vary

There is no single worldwide employment document checklist. Employers and government authorities may have different requirements depending on the country, occupation and employment situation.

Always confirm current requirements with the employer and the appropriate official labour, immigration or government authority before submitting important documents or paying official fees.
